WHAT REMAINS is emission of the consumer, and c262 removed the obstacle that

IT DOES NOT. This NAME-FREE AND COUNT-FREE replacement for the whole
non-challenge branch compiles EXIT 0, and deleting its first line is REJECTED
("invalid last instruction"), so it is load-bearing rather than a no-op:

    do ? (rcondf{1} ^if; first by auto => /#).
    wp; call (_: true).      (* x8 -- one per call, both sides aligned *)
    ...
    wp; skip => /#.

Three facts make it work, and each was measured:
 * `rcondf{i} ^if` works with an ABSTRACT CALL still in the prefix. The second
   dead guard is the literal `false`, so the hoare-side obligation is trivial and
   `auto` never has to step over the call. That is what removes the need to peel
   the challenger's `decaps` first, which is what forced the numeric `seq 2 1`.
 * with both guards gone the two branches hold the SAME EIGHT CALLS IN THE SAME
   ORDER, so a plain backward ladder covers them all -- including the two
   `decaps` -- and no intermediate result ever has to be NAMED. That is what
   removes `_r00`/`_r10`/`ct0`, the EC-inline-renamed names.
 * `do ?` makes the number of dead guards structural instead of counted, so the
   flat state's disagreement about how many `if`s there are stops mattering.

So the route can be driven off the FLAT STATES, like
`_synth_correctness_decaps_casesplit`, and needs no `oracle_tac_override`. The
remaining hop-specific data are all flat-state readable: the shared-prefix length,
the one-sided `encodesharedsecret` and its `_det` axiom, the field holding its
argument, and the regroup lemma name (`types.probe_concat_regroup`).

Note the emitted coupling writes `RD.pq_keys{2}.`2` where the tactic above writes
`RD.pq_keys.`2{2}`; both are valid EC and mean the same thing.
